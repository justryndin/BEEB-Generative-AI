🐣 What pets give you

Pets arrived in update 2.6.100 and unlock in stages, with state progress — if not everything is available to you, that’s normal. They aren’t decoration but another layer of progression:

  • 1
    A permanent bonus to all heroes. Developing pets gives stats to all heroes — even when the pet isn’t in the squad.
  • 2
    Help in battle. Each squad can take one pet — it doesn’t take a hero slot. In a march it deals damage, boosts defense, heals or applies effects.
Stat transfer example. Pet Belek (Beluga) level 1: 110 attack, 30 defense, 6.90K health. With a 25% transfer, heroes get roughly +27 attack, +7 defense and +1.72K health. The higher the pet’s level, the bigger the permanent bonus.

So the benefit is twofold: leveling up the whole collection and picking a pet for a specific squad. Sometimes a simple pet covers a squad’s weakness better than a rare one.

🥚 Where to get eggs — scouting every day

The main source of eggs is pet scouting. Its missions drop eggs, food and materials. It’s not a one-off promo but a daily activity: after a few weeks, regular scouting gives noticeably more food, materials and new pets.

Daily route: log in → check scouting → sort out pet missions → claim rewards → then everything else.
  • Unfinished missions change the next day, while earned but unclaimed rewards are kept for up to 3 days — don’t forget them for long.
  • Rewards include, for example, “Fruit Platter” — +100 XP for a pet. Scouting both finds new pets and levels up the ones you have.
  • Defeat egg thieves on the scouting map — they give valuable rewards, eggs included.
  • Extra eggs and materials show up in themed events.
  • An egg hatches on a timer. Pets have their own home area: names, games, looks (color variants). Progression — level (food) and training (skills).

🔭 How to improve scouting

  • Turtle passes in the supply shop (weekly and monthly) raise the quality of scouting crates — better loot, more interesting eggs.
  • The Intel Post branch in the Lab: “Filter Upgrade” raises the chance of high-quality intel, “Intel Interception” lowers the chance of low-level missions. It’s a long-term investment.
  • Develop your pets — more missions unlock and reward quality goes up. The better the scouting, the more pets.

Rarity isn’t everything. If your main march lacks defense, a leveled-up defensive pet of lower rarity is more useful than a rare attacker that adds damage where you already have plenty. Same with healing, support and enemy debuffs. Early on, collect several different roles rather than one “rarest” pet — that way you see faster who actually strengthens your lineup.

♻️ Duplicates are good

If an egg gives you a pet you already have, it’s not a failure. Duplicate copies turn into pet marks, which are needed for training and enhancement: stars and stats go up, awakening bonuses unlock.

Example. Astra (SSR) 1★ → 2★: health 2,320 → 2,580, attack 465 → 512, defense 153 → 168. Awakening bonus “Star Veil” — health of deployed heroes +3%.

A new pet expands the collection, while a duplicate of one already working in your squad speeds up its progression. A second Astra or Krepysh is sometimes more useful than a new pet that then sits for a month without resources.

🍎 Feeding — where food goes

Food raises a pet’s level: attack, defense, health and combat power go up.

  • XP to the next level grows unevenly: sometimes a little, sometimes a noticeable jump. For Astra — from 120 XP at level 1 to 2,000 at level 38.
  • Power grows evenly: roughly +7.5–7.7K per level (Astra: about 41K at level 1 → about 323K at level 38). So it’s easy to estimate in advance how much stronger a pet will get.
  • Order: food first to your main march’s pet, then to the second most important, the rest get what’s left.
  • When food is scarce, two well-leveled pets are more useful than seven halfway there. Part of a strong pet’s stats transfers to all heroes — the whole account grows.
  • Don’t spend rare food (pudding, feast) on everyone, especially early on.

🎓 Training — count your resources here

Food raises the level, while training moves the pet further along the progression system and unlocks serious upgrades. It needs pet marks and more valuable materials — and they run out much faster than you’d think: the first levels go easily, then your stock vanishes in a few taps.

  1. One pet — for the main march.
  2. A second — for an alternative task.
  3. The rest — develop calmly, no rush.

Before buying, check what’s actually holding progression back: sometimes it’s not food but training, duplicates or other materials — and the extra food just sits in your inventory.

⚔️ How a pet works in battle

One pet per squad. It doesn’t replace a hero and doesn’t become a sixth fighter: its job is an extra effect:

  • extra damage or an attack boost;
  • higher defense, healing;
  • lowering enemy stats, marks;
  • support for certain heroes or rows.

The right question isn’t “which pet is the strongest?” but “what does my squad need right now?”:

  • 🛡
    The front row falls fast under focus → defense. A couple of extra seconds of tank life gives more than an attack bonus: your damage dealers live long enough to use their skills.
  • 💚
    Fights drag on and the team slowly loses health → healing. But if the front collapses in a couple of seconds, healing alone won’t save it — you need baseline survivability first.
  • ⚔️
    You’re a couple of seconds short of finishing off a dangerous target → an attack pet or a debuff. Attackers shine where survivability is already in order.
  • 🎯
    Debuffs and finishing work through the enemy: lower defense, increase damage taken, apply marks. The pet itself hits for little, but if the debuff makes your three main heroes hit noticeably harder, the benefit to the march is very high.

👑 Astra — the most versatile pick

A legendary (SSR) defense pet. Useful almost always: you don’t need to swap her for every opponent. If you keep one well-developed pet for the long run, it’s Astra.

  • Star Wish (main skill): at the start of battle, allied front-row heroes’ defense +20%, back-row heroes’ attack +8% for 10 sec.
  • Star Veil (training stage 2): health of deployed heroes +3%.
  • Star Grace (stage 4): skill damage taken by deployed heroes −2.5%.
  • Star Blessing (stage 5): pet stats +10%.
Radiant Form. A separate stage: it needs a rare special material, and the transformation is irreversible. Gives +5% to the main skill’s effect, +450 attack, +90 defense and +30K health — and through stat transfer it boosts all heroes. Don’t rush: first make sure Astra is definitely staying your main pet.

🐗 Krepysh — against a tough front line

SR, attack and damage. Its strength is pressuring the enemy and working against defense. Especially good in teams with strong opening damage: when the enemy keeps surviving “by a hair”, weakening their defense gives more than a stat bonus.

  • Armored Charge (main skill): at the start of battle, hits the nearest enemy for 600% of the pet’s attack and lowers their defense by 25% for 10 sec.
  • Charging Spirit (stage 2): attack of deployed front-row heroes +3.5%.
  • Line Breaker (stage 4): damage to enemy front-row heroes +3.5%.
  • Pack Leader Instinct (stage 5): pet stats +5%.

🐾 The other pets — in short

  • 🐺
    Dymok — for aggressive lineups: marks, damage focus, finishing. Good against teams that rely on a single key hero. Try it in PvP.
  • 🐢
    Torpeda — defense. If you lose at the very start of battle, the problem isn’t damage: the front dies before the back row gets to act. Boost survivability first.
  • 🐑
    Pushok — healing and support in long fights (especially PvE). If your tanks drop from the first skill burst, healing won’t save them; put it in sturdy teams.
  • 🦝
    Dodo — low-key support, doesn’t need a special team. Not the first candidate for rare materials, but steady value is sometimes better than a “flashy” skill.
  • 🦭
    Belek — simple extra damage. Good enough to start: put it in and level it to a sensible level; when something stronger shows up, swap it out.

🗺 Progression plan from scratch

  1. Unlock as many pets as you can and collect different roles.
  2. Pick a main pet for your main march (the all-rounder is Astra).
  3. Level it up with food, then — training and rare materials.
  4. Develop a second one for another task — defense or healing.
  5. Don’t abandon the rest (the collection gives a bonus too), but don’t make a star out of each one either.
  6. Keep resources for events — for example, “Pet Journey”: feeding and training give rewards there, but only on its days.

⚠️ Common mistakes

  • Don’t speed up eggs right away. Pets are a long game. It hurts to spend speedups right before an event that rewards the same actions. Save speedups for events.
  • Don’t feed everyone. First decide who you’re developing — otherwise food and rare materials get spread thin.
  • An R pet isn’t useless, and an SSR isn’t a reason to give it everything you’ve saved for two weeks right away. Look at its role in the squad and the permanent bonus.
  • Spend money only when it’s clear exactly who you’re developing.

📅 Pets and Alliance Duel

Since 2.6.200, the second day of Alliance Duel has pet development tasks (in states where the system is unlocked). Save food and materials — spend them on the second Duel day to get both the bonus and the points.
